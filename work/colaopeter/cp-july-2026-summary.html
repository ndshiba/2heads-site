Overview

July was the highest-traffic month of the year, with total sessions up 37% from June (2,041 → 2,786). The growth came almost entirely from social channels: Organic Social nearly doubled to 996 sessions and Paid Social (Meta) more than doubled to 718, together making up 62% of all traffic this month. That mix shift is why the site-wide engagement rate dropped to 20.3% from June's 30.8% — it's a denominator effect, not a quality decline. Organic Search held essentially flat at 412 sessions with a strong 64% engagement rate, and consultation-click conversions were 6 for the month, driven mostly by organic search and direct traffic.

✅ Organic Search is still the highest-quality channel on the site - 412 sessions at 64% engagement, essentially unchanged from June's 468 at 65%. Search remains the reliable, consistent channel even as social volume swings month to month.

Organic Social nearly doubled month over month (548 → 996), driven by continued Instagram/Facebook referral activity. Paid Social (Meta ads) more than doubled too (281 → 718) — the campaign that launched in June is scaling up. Both channels carry low engagement by design (5-6%), which is normal for top-of-funnel social discovery and not a red flag on its own. The question worth tracking is whether any of this volume converts later via direct visits or search — that pattern won't be visible until a visitor returns.

AI assistant traffic (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) brought 9 sessions at 56% engagement this month, down from June's 14 at 62%. Volume is small either way, but the quality signal is consistent — AI-referred visitors are highly engaged relative to the site average.

Clicks and impressions both dipped from June, while CTR and average position both improved slightly. That combination — fewer total opportunities to be seen, but slightly better performance where the site did show up — is a mild version of the same pattern seen in May and June's YoY comparisons. It's most likely a seasonal dip (outdoor living searches often soften mid-summer once projects are already underway), but it hasn't been confirmed against a full seasonal baseline yet, since GSC history on this property doesn't go back a full year. Worth a check-in if August doesn't recover.

Estimated organic traffic reached 318 in July — up 218% year over year from 100 in July 2025, and up 14% from June's 279. This is happening alongside a continued narrowing of the keyword footprint: 378 ranking keywords in July, down from June's 518 and February's peak of 594. The pattern that's held since spring continues — fewer total rankings, but the ones that remain are better-targeted and driving more real traffic. 75 keywords are now in the top 10 (30 in positions 1–3), up from June's 60.

Top non-branded rankings in July: "luxury custom pools" (#2), "luxury pool contractors" (#3), and "deck builder washington dc" (#4) — all on 260/month search volume, all in the pool/deck categories where C&P's content is deepest and most differentiated from local competitors.

Recommended Next Steps
1

Watch the GSC click/impression dip into August

July's drop in clicks and impressions (down 18% and 25% from June) is most likely seasonal, but one month isn't enough to confirm that. If August shows the same pattern, it's worth pulling a page-level breakdown to see whether the drop is broad-based or concentrated on a few high-impression pages.

2

Evaluate whether Paid/Organic Social traffic is converting

Social channels (organic + paid combined) are now 62% of total traffic, up from 41% in June. Volume is real, but engagement is low and none of it is showing up yet in consultation_click conversions (6 total this month, all from Organic Search and Direct). Worth checking in September whether any of this summer's social traffic shows up later as returning direct visits or delayed conversions — that's the real test of whether the social spend is working.

3

Second long-form article — still pending

Same recommendation as June: a second in-depth article (pool houses, pergolas, or outdoor kitchens) following the same pattern as the pool renovations piece, which brought in 26 sessions this month (mostly from the Meta ad campaign) and is now linked from the pool service hub page.
